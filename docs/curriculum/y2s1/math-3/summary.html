MTH 207 — Engineering Mathematics III — Differential Equations & Linear Algebra

MTH 207 · Lesson 01 · First-order equations revisited: separable and linear

Lesson 01First-order equations revisited: separable and linear

Key points

  • First-order ODEs met in engineering come in two useful shapes: separable and linear.
  • A separable equation splits the variables to opposite sides and integrates each independently.
  • A linear equation \( y'+P(x)y=Q(x) \) is solved with the integrating factor \( e^{\int P\,dx} \), which turns the left side into an exact derivative.
  • Recognising which shape you have is the whole decision — the integration afterwards is routine.
  • The solution splits into a transient that decays and a steady state that persists.
  • Reading that split tells you what the system settles to and how long it takes to get there, which is usually what was actually wanted.

MTH 207 · Lesson 01 · First-order equations revisited: separable and linear · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Integration — the engine of both methods.
  • The product rule — makes the integrating factor collapse.
  • Exponentials and logs — the language of first-order decay.

MTH 207 · Lesson 02 · Modeling with first-order equations

Lesson 02Modeling with first-order equations

Key points

  • One balance — accumulation equals input minus output — generates the governing equation for tanks, thermal systems and mixing alike.
  • Newton's law of cooling is that balance applied to heat, giving exponential approach to ambient.
  • The time constant is read straight off the coefficient: it is the reciprocal of the decay rate.
  • After one time constant about 63% of the change is complete; after five, the process is finished for practical purposes.
  • That makes the time constant, not the full solution, the number worth carrying around.
  • Modelling means naming the conserved quantity, drawing the boundary, and writing the balance — the calculus follows from that, not the other way round.

MTH 207 · Lesson 02 · Modeling with first-order equations · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Conservation balances — rate in minus rate out.
  • Unit reasoning — the fastest error check.

MTH 207 · Lesson 03 · Second-order equations: the homogeneous story

Lesson 03Second-order equations: the homogeneous story

Key points

  • A second-order constant-coefficient ODE reduces to a quadratic characteristic equation, turning calculus into algebra.
  • The discriminant sorts the response into overdamped, critically damped and underdamped.
  • Critical damping returns fastest without overshoot, which is why instrument and door-closer damping targets it.
  • Recast in standard form, the whole behaviour is captured by natural frequency \( \omega_n \) and damping ratio \( \zeta \).
  • \( \omega_n \) sets how fast, \( \zeta \) sets the shape — and two numbers describe the entire system.
  • That compression is why the same pair of parameters recurs in vibration, in circuits and in every first-cut control model.

MTH 207 · Lesson 03 · Second-order equations: the homogeneous story · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • The quadratic formula — the characteristic equation is a quadratic.
  • Complex numbers — \(e^{i\omega t}=\cos\omega t+i\sin\omega t\).
  • Exponential solutions — the ansatz \(x=e^{rt}\).

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use
Characteristic eqn\( mr^2+cr+k=0 \)Roots set the whole response.
Discriminant\( \Delta=c^2-4mk \)Sign selects the regime.
Natural frequency\( \omega_n=\sqrt{k/m} \)Undamped oscillation rate.
Damping ratio\( \zeta=\frac{c}{2\sqrt{mk}} \)\(<1\) under, \(=1\) critical, \(>1\) over.
Critical damping\( c_c=2\sqrt{mk} \)Fastest return without overshoot.

MTH 207 · Lesson 04 · Nonhomogeneous equations and forced response

Lesson 04Nonhomogeneous equations and forced response

Key points

  • The general solution of a forced linear ODE is the homogeneous solution plus one particular solution — the structure theorem.
  • The homogeneous part is the system's own behaviour; the particular part is its answer to the input.
  • Undetermined coefficients guesses a particular solution of the same form as the forcing and solves for the constants.
  • The homogeneous part decays and is the transient; the particular part persists and is the steady state.
  • Under sinusoidal forcing the steady state is a sinusoid at the same frequency, differing in amplitude and phase.
  • If the forcing matches a homogeneous solution the guess fails and must be multiplied by \( t \) — the algebraic signature of resonance.

MTH 207 · Lesson 04 · Nonhomogeneous equations and forced response · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Matching coefficients — the algebra that fixes \(x_p\).

MTH 207 · Lesson 05 · Free and forced oscillations

Lesson 05Free and forced oscillations

Key points

  • The mass-spring-damper is the equation dynamics keeps returning to, and every result here transfers to circuits and structures.
  • Undamped, it oscillates forever at \( \omega_n=\sqrt{k/m} \); damping makes the ring decay.
  • Forcing near \( \omega_n \) produces resonance, where amplitude is limited only by damping.
  • Two defences exist: move the natural frequency away from the forcing, or add damping to cap the peak.
  • Changing stiffness or mass moves the frequency; damping only limits the amplitude at it.
  • Beats appear when two close frequencies interfere, producing a slow amplitude modulation — audible on machinery and diagnostic of two near-identical sources.

MTH 207 · Lesson 05 · Free and forced oscillations · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Second-order homogeneous solutions — the transient skeleton from Lesson 3.
  • Undetermined coefficients — how the forced steady part is found (Lesson 4).
  • Trigonometric addition — the source of the beat envelope.

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use
Natural frequency\( \omega_n=\sqrt{k/m} \)Undamped ringing rate; set by mass and stiffness alone.
Damped frequency\( \omega_d=\omega_n\sqrt{1-\zeta^2} \)Actual oscillation rate with damping present.
Steady amplitude\( X=\frac{F_0}{\sqrt{(k-m\omega^2)^2+(c\omega)^2}} \)Size of the forced response after the transient.
Resonance\( \omega\to\omega_n \)Amplitude peaks; the case to design away from.
Beat frequency\( |\omega_1-\omega_2| \)Slow pulsing when two near-equal tones add.

MTH 207 · Lesson 06 · Laplace transforms I

Lesson 06Laplace transforms I

Key points

  • The Laplace transform converts a differential equation in \( t \) into an algebraic one in \( s \).
  • The table is built from the defining integral, and a handful of entries covers most engineering work.
  • Linearity lets a transform be assembled piece by piece.
  • The derivative rule \( \mathcal{L}\{y'\}=sY(s)-y(0) \) is what makes the method work, and it absorbs the initial conditions automatically.
  • The shifting theorems handle exponential decay and time delay, which is how dead time gets modelled.
  • Discontinuous and impulsive inputs are handled cleanly, which is precisely where classical methods become awkward.

MTH 207 · Lesson 06 · Laplace transforms I · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Improper integrals — the transform is one, evaluated to \( \infty \).
  • Integration by parts — how the \( t^n \) entries are derived.
  • Exponentials — the kernel \( e^{-st} \) and the shifting theorem.

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use
Definition\( F(s)=\int_0^\infty e^{-st}f\,dt \)The transform itself; rarely computed by hand after 2.
Basic pairs\( 1\!\to\!\tfrac1s,\ e^{at}\!\to\!\tfrac1{s-a},\ t^n\!\to\!\tfrac{n!}{s^{n+1}} \)Look-up table for building \( F(s) \).
Sinusoids\( \cos\omega t\!\to\!\tfrac{s}{s^2+\omega^2},\ \sin\omega t\!\to\!\tfrac{\omega}{s^2+\omega^2} \)Oscillatory terms.
Derivative rule\( \mathcal{L}\{f'\}=sF-f(0) \)Turns an ODE into algebra; folds in initial data.
First shift\( \mathcal{L}\{e^{at}f\}=F(s-a) \)Exponential-times-known-function, no new integral.

MTH 207 · Lesson 07 · Laplace transforms II: solving problems

Lesson 07Laplace transforms II: solving problems

Key points

  • The recipe is three steps: transform the equation, solve for \( Y(s) \) algebraically, invert back to the time domain.
  • Partial fractions is the inversion engine — it breaks \( Y(s) \) into terms the table recognises.
  • Distinct real roots, repeated roots and complex-conjugate pairs each have their own form, and complex pairs signal oscillation.
  • The method earns its keep on problems with initial conditions, step or impulse inputs, and systems of equations.
  • The denominator's roots are the poles, and they determine stability and speed on sight.
  • Reading the answer means recognising which term is transient and which is steady state before evaluating anything.

Assumed knowledge

MTH 207 · Lesson 08 · Systems of equations: the matrix view

Lesson 08Systems of equations: the matrix view

Key points

  • A page of simultaneous equations becomes the single object \( A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b} \).
  • The matrix is a transformation: it maps the unknown vector to the known one.
  • A non-zero determinant means a unique solution exists.
  • A zero determinant means the equations are not independent — physically, a mechanism, a redundant loop, or a missing constraint.
  • The inverse solves the system formally, but elimination is what is used in practice.
  • The matrix view scales: the same notation handles three equations or three thousand, which is what makes numerical methods possible at all.

MTH 207 · Lesson 08 · Systems of equations: the matrix view · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Simultaneous equations — the systems being repackaged.
  • Vectors — the unknown and right-hand side as columns.
  • Arithmetic of rows and columns — the dot products inside \( A\mathbf{x} \).

Glossary

Matrix–vector productrow\(_i\cdot\mathbf{x}=b_i\)Apply or check a solution.

MTH 207 · Lesson 09 · Matrices and linear systems

Lesson 09Matrices and linear systems

Key points

  • Gaussian elimination is the universal method — it works whether or not an inverse exists, and it is what software actually does.
  • Row operations reduce the system to upper triangular form, then back substitution finishes it.
  • Pivots are the leading non-zero entries, and the number of them is the rank.
  • Full rank means a unique solution; deficient rank means either no solution or infinitely many.
  • Partial pivoting — swapping rows to use the largest available pivot — is what keeps the arithmetic numerically stable.
  • Elimination is the engine underneath determinants, inverses and rank, so it is the one procedure worth being fluent in.

MTH 207 · Lesson 10 · Eigenvalues and eigenvectors

Lesson 10Eigenvalues and eigenvectors

Key points

  • An eigenvector is a direction the matrix only stretches: \( A\mathbf{v}=\lambda\mathbf{v} \).
  • Eigenvalues come from \( \det(A-\lambda I)=0 \), and each then yields its eigenvector from a singular system.
  • Engineers compute them because they are a system's natural modes — vibration mode shapes, principal stresses, principal axes of inertia.
  • Diagonalising decouples a coupled system into independent one-degree-of-freedom problems.
  • The spectrum reads a system's fate: negative real parts decay, positive grow, imaginary parts oscillate.
  • That is why stability analysis is an eigenvalue question in every field that uses it.

MTH 207 · Lesson 10 · Eigenvalues and eigenvectors · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Determinants — the singularity condition \( \det(A-\lambda I)=0 \).
  • The quadratic formula — for the \( 2\times2 \) characteristic equation.

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use

MTH 207 · Lesson 11 · Numerical solution of differential equations

Lesson 11Numerical solution of differential equations

Key points

  • Most real ODEs have no closed-form solution, so numerical integration is the normal case rather than the fallback.
  • Euler's method steps along the slope at the current point; it is simple and its error accumulates as \( O(h) \).
  • Heun's method averages the slope at both ends of the step and improves the order to \( O(h^2) \).
  • Fourth-order Runge–Kutta samples four slopes per step for \( O(h^4) \) accuracy, which is why it is the default workhorse.
  • Smaller steps cut truncation error but cost more evaluations and eventually accumulate round-off — accuracy is traded against cost.
  • Stability is separate from accuracy: a stiff system can go numerically unstable at a step size that looks perfectly reasonable, which is what implicit methods exist to fix.

MTH 207 · Lesson 11 · Numerical solution of differential equations · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Initial-value problems — a starting point plus \( y'=f(x,y) \).
