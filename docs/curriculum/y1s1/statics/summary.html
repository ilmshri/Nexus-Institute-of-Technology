STA 103 — Engineering Statics

STA 103 · Lesson 01 · Forces, vectors, and resultants

Lesson 01Forces, vectors, and resultants

Key points

  • A force is a vector: magnitude, direction and line of action. The arrow on the diagram is a contract about all three.
  • Weight is not mass. \( W = mg \) converts one to the other; kilograms are mass, newtons are force, and the distinction survives every later course.
  • Resolve into components to add, then recombine with \( R = \sqrt{R_x^2 + R_y^2} \) and a direction angle.
  • Cables pull only; struts can also push. What a member can carry is part of the geometry.
  • Working method: choose axes that kill the most unknowns — usually one axis along an unknown force.

STA 103 · Lesson 01 · Forces, vectors, and resultants · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Trigonometry — sine, cosine, tangent on right triangles, fluently and without a diagram panic.
  • SI units — newtons, kilograms, and the prefix ladder (kN, MPa come soon).
  • Vector idea — a quantity with direction; formal vector algebra is built here, not assumed.

Glossary

STA 103 · Lesson 02 · Moments and couples

Lesson 02Moments and couples

Key points

  • A moment is the turning effect of a force: magnitude times perpendicular distance to the point.
  • Varignon's theorem: the moment of a force equals the sum of the moments of its components — so you may replace an awkward perpendicular distance with convenient component distances.
  • A couple is two equal, opposite, non-collinear forces: pure rotation, no net force, and the same moment about every point in the plane.
  • Working method: choose the moment centre to eliminate unknowns — put it where the forces you do not want to solve for intersect.

STA 103 · Lesson 02 · Moments and couples · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Force components (Lesson 1) — Varignon's theorem is useless without quick resolution.
  • Perpendicular distance — reading the shortest distance from a point to a line off a sketch.

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use

Cross-product form\( \mathbf{M} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F} \)3-D moments, or when the perpendicular distance is awkward to see.

Couple\( M = F\,s \) (force pair, spacing \( s \))Pure turning with zero net force; the same moment about EVERY point.

STA 103 · Lesson 03 · The free-body diagram

Lesson 03The free-body diagram

Key points

  • Isolate the body, replace every contact with the force it can transmit, then label everything. Nothing else.
  • Supports are force vocabularies: a roller supplies one force, a pin two, a fixed support two plus a moment. Learn the menu and the diagram writes itself.
  • Practically every equilibrium error is a free-body-diagram error, not an algebra error.
  • Never write \( \Sigma F = 0 \) until the picture is finished.

STA 103 · Lesson 03 · The free-body diagram · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Forces as vectors (Lesson 1) — reactions are drawn as components.
  • Moments (Lesson 2) — fixed supports supply one, and \( \Sigma M = 0 \) will need it.

Glossary

STA 103 · Lesson 04 · Equilibrium of a particle

Lesson 04Equilibrium of a particle

Key points

  • Concurrent forces act through one point, so only force balance is available: \( \Sigma F_x = 0 \) and \( \Sigma F_y = 0 \) (add \( \Sigma F_z = 0 \) in three dimensions).
  • There is no moment equation here, and none is needed — every line of action passes through the same point.
  • Components or geometry (the closed force triangle) are both valid; choose deliberately rather than by habit.
  • An ideal cable over a frictionless pulley carries the same tension along its whole length.

STA 103 · Lesson 04 · Equilibrium of a particle · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Resolution into components (Lesson 1) — the working method of the whole lesson.
  • FBD discipline (Lesson 3) — the node is isolated and every cable drawn pulling.
  • Simultaneous equations — two unknowns from two linear equations, cleanly.

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use
Particle equilibrium\( \Sigma F_x = 0,\ \Sigma F_y = 0 \)Concurrent force systems — rings, hooks, connection points.
Two-force equilibriumequal, opposite, collinearA body under exactly two forces; the shortcut behind two-force members.
Cable assumptiontension only, along the cableCables pull; a computed compression in a cable means the model is wrong.

Equilibrium checksubstitute backVerify both component sums close to zero with the solved values.

STA 103 · Lesson 05 · Equilibrium of rigid bodies

Lesson 05Equilibrium of rigid bodies

Key points

  • A rigid body in a plane obeys three independent equations: \( \Sigma F_x = 0 \), \( \Sigma F_y = 0 \), \( \Sigma M = 0 \) about any point you choose.
  • Two-force members carry force along the line joining their two connection points. Spotting them collapses the algebra before it starts.
  • Count unknowns against equations: more unknowns than equations means statically indeterminate, and statics alone cannot finish the problem.

STA 103 · Lesson 05 · Equilibrium of rigid bodies · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • FBDs and support menus (Lesson 3) — reactions drawn correctly is the entry fee.
  • Moments and Varignon (Lesson 2) — \( \Sigma M_O = 0 \) is the workhorse equation.
  • Particle equilibrium (Lesson 4) — the force-balance half of the toolkit.

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use
Rigid-body equilibrium\( \Sigma F_x = 0,\ \Sigma F_y = 0,\ \Sigma M = 0 \)Bodies with size — beams, frames, brackets; three equations, no more.
Pin support2 unknowns \( (A_x, A_y) \)Resists translation both ways, spins freely.
Roller support1 unknown, \( \perp \) surfaceResists only normal to its rolling surface.
Fixed support3 unknowns \( (A_x, A_y, M_A) \)Welded or embedded ends; adds a reaction moment.

STA 103 · Lesson 06 · Trusses

Lesson 06Trusses

Key points

  • A truss is a set of two-force members: axial load only, tension or compression, no bending.
  • Method of joints when you need every member; method of sections when you need a few members deep inside the truss without working through it joint by joint.
  • Zero-force members carry nothing in this load case but hold geometry and resist other cases — recognise them to simplify, never to delete them.

STA 103 · Lesson 06 · Trusses · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Particle equilibrium (Lesson 4) — a joint is exactly that problem, repeated.
  • Rigid-body equilibrium (Lesson 5) — support reactions come first; sections use \( \Sigma M \).
  • Two-force members (Lesson 5) — the fact that makes the whole idealization work.

Glossary

STA 103 · Lesson 07 · Frames and machines

Lesson 07Frames and machines

Key points

  • Frames are not trusses: they contain multi-force members, so the two-force shortcut does not apply.
  • Dismember at the pins and pay Newton his due — the force one member exerts on another appears equal and opposite on the mating diagram. Getting that sign wrong silently violates equilibrium for the pin itself.
  • Machines are frames built to multiply force; mechanical advantage falls out of the same equilibrium equations, with no new theory.

STA 103 · Lesson 07 · Frames and machines · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Rigid-body equilibrium (Lesson 5) — applied member by member.
  • Two-force member recognition (Lesson 5) — the frame analyst's first move.
  • Truss thinking (Lesson 6) — to see when it does not apply.

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use
Framecontains ≥1 multi-force memberStructures with members carrying 3+ forces; joints solved by disassembly.
Machineframe with moving partsPliers, clamps, linkages — force multiplication devices.
Member disassemblyFBD per memberThe frame method: pull the pins apart and expose the pin forces.
Pin-force pairsequal and opposite on the two membersNewton's third law at every shared pin — the bookkeeping backbone.
Two-force member shortcutforce along the member lineSpot them first; each one turns two unknowns into one.
Mechanical advantage\( F_{out}/F_{in} = a_{in}/a_{out} \)Lever-arm ratios in machines — what the tool multiplies.

STA 103 · Lesson 08 · Internal forces: shear and bending moment

Lesson 08Internal forces: shear and bending moment

Key points

  • Cut the beam and three internal resultants restore equilibrium on the exposed face: normal force \( N \), shear force \( V \), bending moment \( M \).
  • The sign convention is arbitrary; consistency is not. Fix one and keep it for life.
  • The calculus that draws the diagrams: \( dV/dx = -w(x) \) and \( dM/dx = V \), where \( w \) is the distributed load intensity in N/m.
  • Between point loads \( V \) is constant and \( M \) runs straight; under uniform load \( V \) slopes and \( M \) curves. A point load steps \( V \); a couple steps \( M \).
  • The design headline: the bending moment is extremal where the shear crosses zero.

STA 103 · Lesson 08 · Internal forces: shear and bending moment · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Support reactions (Lessons 3, 5) — computed before any cut is made.
  • Equilibrium of a cut body (Lesson 5) — the exposed section is just another FBD.
  • Basic calculus — slopes and areas connect \( w \), \( V \), and \( M \).

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use
Internal forcesN, V, M at a cutWhat the material itself must carry at any section.
Method of sections (beams)cut, keep one side, apply equilibriumN, V, M at one location in one pass.
Shear diagram\( V(x) \)How transverse load accumulates along the span.
Moment diagram\( M(x) \)Where bending is worst — the beam designer's map.
Diagram calculus\( dV/dx = -w,\ dM/dx = V \)Slopes and areas connect load, shear, and moment; V = 0 locates max M.

STA 103 · Lesson 09 · Friction

Lesson 09Friction

Key points

  • Dry friction has a threshold: \( F \le \mu_s N \). Equality holds only at impending motion — below it, friction is simply whatever equilibrium demands, no more.
  • That inequality is the single most misused relation in statics: friction is not automatically \( \mu_s N \).
  • Wedges and screws are friction devices, and are self-locking when the friction angle exceeds the lead angle — which is why a screw jack holds its load.
  • Belt friction is exponential in wrap angle, so a modest increase in wrap multiplies holding force. One extra turn round a capstan changes the problem.

STA 103 · Lesson 09 · Friction · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Equilibrium with inequalities in mind (Lesson 5) — friction saturates; it is not always \( \mu N \).
  • FBD discipline (Lesson 3) — friction direction set by impending motion, drawn before solving.
  • Exponentials — the capstan equation lives on \( e^{\mu\beta} \).

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use
Static friction\( F_s \le \mu_s N \)Adaptive holding force up to a ceiling; equality only at impending slip.
Kinetic friction\( F_k = \mu_k N \)Once sliding, friction drops to this steady value.
Impending motion\( F_s = \mu_s N \)The boundary case where friction problems are solved.
Angle of repose\( \tan\phi = \mu_s \)The steepest slope a body rests on; a field measurement of \( \mu_s \).
Slip vs tipcompare \( \mu_s N \) with the tipping ratioTall loads may tip before they slide; check both failure modes.
Normal-force honestyN from equilibrium, not assumptionPushes and pulls change N — recompute it before multiplying by \( \mu \).

STA 103 · Lesson 10 · Centroids and centers of gravity

Lesson 10Centroids and centers of gravity

Key points

  • A centroid is the average position of area; a centre of gravity is the average position of weight. They coincide only when the material is uniform.
  • Tabular method: one row per simple part, columns for \( A \), \( \bar{x} \), \( \bar{y} \), then the products. Holes enter as negative area — the arithmetic handles them without special cases.
  • Replace area with weight and the same table returns the centre of gravity, which is where the lifting hook must sit if the load is to hang level.

STA 103 · Lesson 10 · Centroids and centers of gravity · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Basic integration — (1) is used directly only for curved shapes; know what the integral means.
  • Moments (Lesson 2) — \( A\bar{x} \) is a first moment; the language is identical.
  • Weight vs mass (Lesson 1) — CG tables may carry kg; convert before forces are needed.

STA 103 · Lesson 11 · Moments of inertia

Lesson 11Moments of inertia

Key points

  • The second moment of area is area times distance squared: a section's resistance to bending, not a mass property.
  • Parallel-axis theorem: \( I = \bar{I} + Ad^2 \). The transfer term is squared in distance, so it usually dominates.
  • That squared distance is why sections look the way they do: an I-beam puts its material as far from the neutral axis as it can, and a tube beats a solid rod of equal weight.

STA 103 · Lesson 11 · Moments of inertia · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Centroids (Lesson 10) — the composite centroid must be located before any \( d \) is measured.
  • Composite bookkeeping (Lesson 10) — the same table grows two more columns.
  • Integration — (1) is evaluated once for the rectangle; after that, tables and transfer.

Glossary

Rectangle about base\( I = bh^3/3 \)When the reference axis sits on an edge.
