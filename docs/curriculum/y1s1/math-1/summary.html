MTH 101 — Engineering Mathematics I

MTH 101 · Lesson 01 · Functions, units, and engineering magnitudes

Lesson 01Functions, units, and engineering magnitudes

Key points

  • Every physical quantity carries a unit, and units obey algebra exactly as numbers do.
  • Dimensional homogeneity: both sides of a physical equation must carry identical dimensions, and the arguments of \( \sin \), \( \ln \) and \( e^x \) must be dimensionless. Checking this kills roughly half of all calculation errors before they are made.
  • A function is a machine's behaviour written down — the pump curve \( H(Q) \), the torque curve \( T(\omega) \), the cooling curve \( T(t) \). The domain is physical, not decorative: beyond runout flow the curve is forbidden, not merely undefined.
  • Estimate before you calculate. If a suitcase-sized transfer pump computes to 300 kW, the arithmetic is wrong — and you know it before re-checking.
  • Working method: substitute units rather than numbers and confirm both sides match; convert every input to base SI before any arithmetic; convert the answer back to trade units and test it against an order-of-magnitude estimate.
  • Two traps with names: the gauge trap (gauge and absolute pressure differ by one atmosphere) and the prefix trap (kPa meeting Pa, m³/h meeting m³/s in the same line).

MTH 101 · Lesson 01 · Functions, units, and engineering magnitudes · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Algebraic manipulation — solving \( P=\rho g Q H \) for any one symbol.
  • SI prefixes — k, M, m, µ span nine orders of magnitude; conversion is exponent bookkeeping.
  • Scientific notation — \( 3.2\times10^4 \) beats 32000 for error-catching.

MTH 101 · Lesson 02 · Limits and continuity

Lesson 02Limits and continuity

Key points

  • A limit describes what a quantity approaches, which is not always what it equals at the point itself.
  • Most plant quantities are continuous, but not all — some genuinely jump, and pretending otherwise hides real behaviour.
  • One-sided limits are the honest description of a switching event: a relay closing, a valve seating, a pump cutting in.
  • The ratio \( \Delta y/\Delta x \) as \( \Delta x \to 0 \) is why limits matter at all — it is the derivative in embryo.
  • Evaluation order: substitute first; if that gives \( 0/0 \), factor, rationalise or expand and try again; for behaviour at large argument, compare the dominant terms.

MTH 101 · Lesson 02 · Limits and continuity · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Function evaluation and graphs (Lesson 1) — reading \( f \) near a point, not just at it.
  • Radian measure — the sine limit is false in degrees; radians make arc = radius × angle.
  • Factoring — resolving \( \tfrac{x^2-9}{x-3} \) requires \( (x-3)(x+3) \).

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use
Limit\( \lim_{x\to a} f(x) = L \)The settling value; approach, not arrival — may exist where the function value doesn't.
The sine limit\( \lim_{\theta\to 0}\tfrac{\sin\theta}{\theta} = 1 \)Small-angle work and the derivative of sine; radians only.
The 0/0 formfactor, or multiply by the conjugateWhen substitution returns 0/0, a hidden common factor is waiting to cancel.
Continuity\( \lim_{x\to a} f(x) = f(a) \)Deciding whether a logged jump can be physical or must be instrumental.
One-sided limits\( x\to a^-,\ x\to a^+ \)Trip points, hysteresis, absolute values, piecewise behavior.
Removable discontinuityhole at \( x=a \)The limit exists; the definition merely misses the point.

MTH 101 · Lesson 03 · The derivative as a rate

Lesson 03The derivative as a rate

Key points

  • The derivative is the instantaneous rate of change — the average rate over an interval that has been allowed to shrink to nothing.
  • Read the sign and the magnitude before the formula: sign gives direction of drift, magnitude gives urgency.
  • From a model: differentiate the expression. From data: take finite differences — but note that shrinking the interval amplifies measurement noise, so the smallest interval is rarely the best one.
  • The units of a derivative are always (units of the output) divided by (units of the input). This alone identifies what a rate physically is.

MTH 101 · Lesson 03 · The derivative as a rate · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Limits (Lesson 2) — the derivative is literally a limit; no limit, no slope.
  • Slope of a line — rise over run; the derivative generalizes it to curves.
  • Function notation (Lesson 1) — \( T'(t) \) is itself a new function of time.

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use
Derivative\( \tfrac{dT}{dt} = \lim_{\Delta t\to 0}\tfrac{\Delta T}{\Delta t} \)The instantaneous rate; the slope of the trend at one instant, always with units.
Secant (average) rate\( \Delta T/\Delta t \)What two spot readings give; the derivative's finite approximation.
Forward difference\( (f_{i+1} - f_i)/h \)Rate from the latest two samples — the only choice at the record's live edge.
Central difference\( (f_{i+1} - f_{i-1})/(2h) \)The better mid-record estimate on smooth data; first-order errors cancel.
Rate-to-deadlinesolve \( f'(t) = \text{limit} \)Turning an alarm rate into a time-to-act.
Differentiabilityno corners, no jumpsKnowing where a slope exists at all; trend corners mark regime changes.

MTH 101 · Lesson 04 · Differentiation rules and the chain rule

Lesson 04Differentiation rules and the chain rule

Key points

  • Constant-multiple, sum, power, product and quotient rules: each is proved once from the limit definition and then used for life.
  • Chain rule: plant variables cause one another in chains — valve position sets flow, flow sets level, level sets pressure. Multiply the local sensitivities along the chain.
  • Relative rates are the engineer's form: divide a derivative by its own function. For the fan law \( P = kN^3 \), logarithmic differentiation gives \( dP/P = 3\,dN/N \) — a 2% speed increase costs about 6% more power.
  • That single result is why trimming fan or pump speed slightly is usually the cheapest energy project available.
  • Choose the rule from the structure of the expression before touching symbols.

MTH 101 · Lesson 04 · Differentiation rules and the chain rule · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • The derivative as limit (Lesson 3) — today's rules are shortcuts to that limit.
  • Exponent algebra — \( x^{-1} \), \( x^{1/2} \) so the power rule covers roots and reciprocals.
  • Composite functions — recognizing \( f(g(x)) \) before differentiating it.

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use
Power rule\( (x^n)' = n\,x^{n-1} \)Sums of powers — after rewriting roots and reciprocals as powers.
Product rule\( (uv)' = u'v + uv' \)Two varying factors multiplied: force × velocity, pressure × flow.
Quotient rule\( (u/v)' = (u'v - uv')/v^2 \)Ratios with the variable below the line; the subtraction order is fixed.
Chain rule\( \tfrac{dy}{dx} = \tfrac{dy}{du}\cdot\tfrac{du}{dx} \)Nested dependence; rates multiply along the cause chain.
Relative sensitivity\( \tfrac{dP}{P} = n\,\tfrac{dN}{N} \) for \( P = kN^n \)Percent-per-percent talk; affinity-law estimates and energy arguments.
Exact vs first-order\( (1+r)^n \) vs \( 1+nr \)Deciding when the linear estimate is honest enough — and checking it when it isn't.

MTH 101 · Lesson 05 · Exponentials, logarithms, and growth/decay

Lesson 05Exponentials, logarithms, and growth/decay

Key points

  • \( e^x \) is its own derivative; exponential behaviour is what happens whenever the rate of change is proportional to the amount currently present.
  • The plant's standard curve is decay toward an offset, not toward zero: something approaches a final value \( y_\infty \) rather than vanishing.
  • The time constant \( \tau \) is the whole story: about 63% of the journey is complete after one \( \tau \), 95% after three, 99% after five.
  • Logarithms are how you solve for time — "when will it reach X" is a logarithm, every time.
  • Transient protocol: identify the initial value, the final value, and \( \tau \). With those three, the curve is fully determined.

MTH 101 · Lesson 05 · Exponentials, logarithms, and growth/decay · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Chain rule (Lesson 4) — \( \tfrac{d}{dt}e^{-t/\tau} = -\tfrac{1}{\tau}e^{-t/\tau} \).
  • Log laws — \( \ln(ab) = \ln a + \ln b \), \( \ln e^x = x \).
  • Limits (Lesson 2) — \( e^{-t/\tau}\to 0 \): the transient's destiny is ambient.

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use
Exponential change\( \tfrac{d}{dt}e^{kt} = k\,e^{kt} \)Any quantity whose rate is proportional to its size — growth or decay.
First-order transient\( T = T_\infty + (T_0 - T_\infty)e^{-t/\tau} \)Cooling, charging, draining — decay to an offset destination.
Time constant\( \tau \): 63% per \( \tau \), 95% by \( 3\tau \)Reading any transient's tempo at a glance.
Solving for time\( t = \tau\ln\tfrac{T_0 - T_\infty}{T - T_\infty} \)When the question is when, not what.
Half-life\( t_{1/2} = \tau\ln 2 \approx 0.693\tau \)Halving language for decays; never equal to \( \tau \) itself.
Semilog straightening\( \ln(T - T_\infty) \) vs \( t \), slope \( -1/\tau \)Measuring \( \tau \) from data — and testing whether first-order applies at all.
Doubling time\( n = \ln 2/\ln(1+r) \)Compounding costs, loads, and deposits.

MTH 101 · Lesson 06 · Extrema, curve behavior, and optimization

Lesson 06Extrema, curve behavior, and optimization

Key points

  • Curves turn where the derivative vanishes; the second derivative says whether the turn is a maximum or a minimum.
  • Optimization recipe: express the objective in one variable, differentiate, solve for stationary points — then check the endpoints.
  • Checking endpoints is not a formality. Constrained optima frequently sit at a limit rather than at a turning point, and skipping the check is the classic way to report a wrong optimum confidently.
  • The hard part is translation, not calculus: turning a stated goal into an honest objective function with its real constraints.

MTH 101 · Lesson 06 · Extrema, curve behavior, and optimization · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Differentiation rules (Lesson 4) — fast, error-free \( f' \) is the entry fee.
  • Solving equations — critical points come from \( f'(x)=0 \), often polynomial.
  • Geometry formulas — areas and volumes supply objectives and constraints.

MTH 101 · Lesson 07 · The integral as accumulation

Lesson 07The integral as accumulation

Key points

  • The derivative asks how fast; the integral asks how much in total. They are the two halves of the subject.
  • \( V = \int_0^T Q(t)\,dt \) — every totalizer in the plant is performing this calculation continuously.
  • Fundamental Theorem: accumulation and differentiation undo each other, so \( \int_a^b f = F(b) - F(a) \). Find any antiderivative and evaluate at the two ends.
  • Area under a curve is a physical total. Read the units of the area to know which total it is: rate × time is quantity, force × distance is work.

MTH 101 · Lesson 07 · The integral as accumulation · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • The derivative (Lesson 3) — the Fundamental Theorem runs it backwards.
  • Limits of sums (Lesson 2) — the rectangle stack converges by the same logic.
  • Power rule (Lesson 4) — reversed: \( \int x^n dx = x^{n+1}/(n{+}1) + C,\ n\neq -1 \).

Glossary

Definite integral\( V = \int_0^T Q(t)\,dt \)Totals from rates: volume from flow, energy from power, distance from speed.
Antiderivative\( F' = f \)The function you evaluate at the ends; unique up to a constant.
Fundamental Theorem\( \int_a^b f\,dt = F(b) - F(a) \)Computing accumulations without rectangles — and auditing totalizers from two register readings.
Average from a register\( \bar{f} = \tfrac{F(b)-F(a)}{b-a} \)Turning cumulative meter readings into honest average rates.

MTH 101 · Lesson 08 · Integration techniques

Lesson 08Integration techniques

Key points

  • Substitution is the chain rule run backwards — look for a function and its own derivative sitting in the same integrand.
  • Integration by parts is the product rule run backwards: \( \int u\,dv = uv - \int v\,du \). Choose \( u \) to be the factor that simplifies when differentiated.
  • Know when to stop. Many perfectly ordinary integrals have no closed form, and a numerical answer is a legitimate engineering answer, not a defeat.

MTH 101 · Lesson 08 · Integration techniques · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Chain rule (Lesson 4) and product rule — today's methods are their inverses.
  • Antiderivatives (Lesson 7) — the target object of every technique.
  • Exponentials (Lesson 5) — \( \int e^{kt}dt = e^{kt}/k + C \) appears constantly.

Glossary

Substitution\( u = g(x),\ du = g'(x)\,dx \)The inner function's derivative sits beside it, up to a constant.
Integration by parts\( \int u\,dv = uv - \int v\,du \)Genuine products: polynomial × exponential or sine; pick \( u \) to simplify.
Limits under substitution\( x{=}a \to u{=}g(a) \)Transform the limits with the variable and never convert back.
Table formmemorized standardsRecognize before deriving; most plant integrals are three patterns.
Trapezoid rule\( \sum \tfrac{f_i + f_{i+1}}{2}\,\Delta t \)Sampled data — the licensed, honest fallback.

MTH 101 · Lesson 09 · Applications: areas, volumes, work, and averages

Lesson 09Applications: areas, volumes, work, and averages

Key points

  • Average value is the integral spread evenly across the interval. Every KPI beginning with the word "average" is this calculation — whether or not it was computed honestly.
  • Averaging a few spot readings taken at convenient times is not this calculation, and you can now say precisely why.
  • RMS is the average that heats. Electrical heating goes as the square of current, so the meaningful average for an alternating quantity is the root of the mean of the square — which is why vibration severity standards quote RMS velocity.
  • Work by integration when force varies along the path: \( W = \int F\,dx \). Emptying a tank is the signature case, because each layer must rise a different height.
  • Choosing the right average — mean, RMS, or time-weighted — is a field decision with different answers, not a matter of taste.

MTH 101 · Lesson 09 · Applications: areas, volumes, work, and averages · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Definite integrals (Lesson 7) and techniques (Lesson 8).
  • Trig identity — \( \sin^2\theta = \tfrac12(1-\cos 2\theta) \) drives the RMS result.
  • Density and weight — \( \rho g \) converts volume layers to newtons.

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use
Mean value\( \bar f = \tfrac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b f\,dt \)Billing, throughput, duty — "how much per unit time," honestly.
RMS\( \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{T}\int_0^T f^2\,dt} \); sine: \( A/\sqrt2 \)Heating, vibration severity, fatigue — damage physics runs on squares.
Crest factorpeak ÷ RMSImpulsiveness flag: ≈1.4 for a sine, climbing as impacts appear.

Peak\( \max |f| \)Survival of the worst instant: trips, relief, insulation.

MTH 101 · Lesson 10 · First-order differential equations

Lesson 10First-order differential equations

Key points

  • An equation containing its own rate of change describes nearly every tank, thermal mass and mixing problem you will meet.
  • Separation of variables: gather each variable on its own side, integrate both, then apply the initial condition to fix the constant.
  • Put the equation in the standard form \( \dot{y} = -(y - y_\infty)/\tau \) and the time constant can be read straight off the coefficients — tank volume over volumetric flow for a flushed tank, thermal mass over loss coefficient for a cooling body.
  • You can often read the dynamics without solving anything: the final value and the time constant answer most practical questions.
  • Modelling method: start from a balance statement — rate in minus rate out — and express each rate in terms of the state variable.

MTH 101 · Lesson 10 · First-order differential equations · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Integration (Lessons 7–8) — both sides of the solution are integrals.
  • Log/exponential inversion (Lesson 5) — un-logging produces the exponential form.
  • Derivative-as-rate (Lesson 3) — the equation's left side is a trend slope.

The balance template\( \dot{y} = \text{rate in} - \text{rate out} \)Writing the model before solving it; physics enters through the rates.
Separation\( \int\tfrac{dT}{T-T_\infty} = -\int\tfrac{dt}{\tau} \)The solving move for separable equations.

MTH 101 · Lesson 11 · Taylor series and approximation

Lesson 11Taylor series and approximation

Key points

  • Every smooth function is locally a polynomial. That is the entire idea, and it is what makes linearization defensible rather than lazy.
  • The classic small approximations — \( \sin x \approx x \), \( (1+x)^n \approx 1 + nx \), \( e^x \approx 1 + x \) — are all the same theorem truncated after one term.
  • The remainder term is not optional bookkeeping: it is how you state the size of the error you just agreed to accept.
  • A linearization you can defend comes with its range of validity attached. Without that range, it is a guess wearing a formula.

MTH 101 · Lesson 11 · Taylor series and approximation · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Derivatives of all orders (Lessons 3–5) — the series' coefficients are derivatives at a point.
  • Factorials and powers — the series' bookkeeping.
  • Radians (Lesson 2) — the small-angle set is a radian-only privilege.
