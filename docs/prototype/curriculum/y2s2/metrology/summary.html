MET 204 — Metrology & Quality Control

MET 204 · Lesson 01 · Measurement fundamentals

Lesson 01Measurement fundamentals

Key points

  • Resolution is what the display can show, precision is agreement among repeats, bias is a systematic offset, and accuracy needs small bias and small scatter.
  • The worked micrometer reads 25.0052 mm on a 25.0000 mm block with \( s=1.03\ \mu\mathrm{m} \): precise and inaccurate, the combination that survives careless checking.
  • \( u(\bar{x})=s/\sqrt{n} \): ten readings tighten the mean to 0.33 µm and leave the 5.2 µm bias exactly where it was.
  • Judge a check against its reference: a 5.2 µm bias against a block certified to 0.2 µm is 26:1, so the error is the instrument's.
  • Traceability is an unbroken chain of calibrations to a national standard, each with a stated uncertainty; uncertainty accumulates downward and never shrinks.
  • A certificate states measurand, reference standard, result with uncertainty and coverage factor, and conditions. A result without an uncertainty is not a result.
  • Dimensional metrology is defined at 20°C, and \( \Delta L=L\alpha\Delta T \) is not optional arithmetic.
  • A 200 mm steel part at 25°C is 11.5 µm long — a quarter of an IT7 band; in aluminium, 23.1 µm.
  • Measuring aluminium with a steel gauge leaves the differential 11.6 µm, which no instrument quality removes.
  • The 4:1 rule compares tolerance with uncertainty; a known bias belongs corrected, not absorbed into the budget.

MET 204 · Lesson 02 · Dimensional instruments

Lesson 02Dimensional instruments

Key points

  • Calipers are wide-range and least accurate, micrometers trade range for accuracy, dial indicators measure differences only, gauge blocks are embodied length, and a comparator is an indicator plus a stand plus a block.
  • Abbe's principle: the measuring line should be collinear with the scale. Any offset \( h \) turns an angular error into \( \varepsilon = h\tan\theta \).
  • Abbe error is first order in tilt; cosine error \( L(1-\cos\theta) \), all that remains when \( h=0 \), is second order.
  • A caliper with a 40 mm offset tilted 0.1° gives 69.8 µm; a micrometer at the same tilt gives 0.038 µm — a factor of 1833, bought by geometry rather than workmanship.
  • Double the tilt and the caliper error doubles while the micrometer error quadruples from a number that was already negligible.
  • Gauge blocks wring so a stack acts as one length; build from the finest decimal first, and use as few blocks as possible — 41.275 mm as 1.005 + 1.27 + 8.0 + 31.0.
  • Every wrung interface adds uncertainty and a contamination risk, so four blocks beat seven for the same length.
  • A ratchet fixes the measuring force so contact deformation is repeatable and calibration can absorb it; feeling the fit converts a bias into scatter.
  • Parallax, anvil wear, dirt and hand heat are all systematic and none is fixed by buying a better instrument — a specification is an upper bound on performance, not a description of it.
  • An error that depends on technique passes a repeatability check and fails between operators, which is what gauge R&R exists to expose.

MET 204 · Lesson 03 · Uncertainty and calibration

Lesson 03Uncertainty and calibration

Key points

  • Uncertainty is a computed interval, not a mistake or a tolerance; a result without one cannot be used for a decision.
  • Type A and Type B classify how a component was evaluated — by statistics, or by any other means. It is not the random/systematic distinction.
  • Convert to standard uncertainty first: \( s/\sqrt n \) for a mean, \( U/k \) from a certificate, \( a/\sqrt3 \) for a rectangular half-width.
  • The worked micrometer budget: repeatability 0.327, resolution 0.289, temperature 0.166, block 0.100 µm.
  • \( u_c=\sqrt{\sum u_i^2}=0.477\ \mu\mathrm{m} \) and \( U=2u_c=0.95\ \mu\mathrm{m} \); the reported result is a \( -5.2\ \mu\mathrm{m} \) correction with that uncertainty.
  • The bias is 5.5 times \( U \), so it is real and belongs corrected; a bias comparable with \( U \) would honestly be "none detected".
  • Variance shares are the spending plan: 46.9% repeatability, 36.6% resolution, 12.1% temperature, 4.4% block.
  • A ten-times-better block buys 2%; quadrupling the readings buys 19% and then leaves resolution dominant — rebuild the budget after every improvement.
  • Guard banding pulls acceptance limits in by \( U \): a 21 µm tolerance becomes a 19.1 µm band, protecting the customer and rejecting some good parts.
  • A negative guard band means the measurement cannot support the decision — the 4:1 rule of Lesson 1 arriving from the other direction.
  • An out-of-tolerance finding at calibration puts every measurement since the last good calibration in question, which is what records and intervals exist to bound.

MET 204 · Lesson 04 · Form, fits, and GD&T verification

Lesson 04Form, fits, and GD&T verification

Key points

  • Size and form are different quantities: an odd-lobed shaft has constant diameter and varying radius, so every two-point measurement agrees while the part is not round.
  • Form controls (flatness, straightness, roundness, cylindricity) need no datum; related controls (runout, position, orientation) do — which predicts which measurements are hard.
  • A roundness trace's first harmonic is the setup's eccentricity, not the part's shape: \( e=\sqrt{b_1^2+c_1^2} \).
  • The worked trace reads 22 µm peak to valley, of which 11.17 µm at 331° is centring; the genuine out-of-roundness is 2.82 µm, so 87% of it was an artefact.
  • Harmonic content is a diagnosis: two cycles suggests clamping, three suggests centreless grinding, higher suggests chatter. The total alone hides it.
  • Runout is a composite, \( \mathrm{TIR}=2e+\text{form}+\text{datum} \) — an excellent screen and a poor diagnosis.
  • A 0.080 mm TIR with 0.006 mm of form means \( e=0.037 \) mm, which on a 12 kg rotor at 3000 rev/min is 43.8 N of rotating force into the bearings.
  • A position tolerance is a cylindrical zone, so the error is a diameter, \( 2\sqrt{\Delta x^2+\Delta y^2} \) — forgetting the factor of two passes parts that should fail.
  • At MMC the tolerance grows by the departure from MMC: a \( \varnothing10.00 \) hole made at 10.06 earns 0.06, so 0.241 mm of error passes against 0.26 and would have failed against 0.2.
  • A CMM evaluates least-squares substitute geometry fitted to the points it took, so sampling strategy is part of the method and its own uncertainty belongs in the Lesson 3 budget.

MET 204 · Lesson 05 · Limit gauging and Taylor's principle

Lesson 05Limit gauging and Taylor's principle

Key points

  • A limit gauge is a limit built into steel: the GO gauge embodies maximum material condition, the NO-GO gauge least material condition.
  • For a hole MMC is the smallest permitted size and for a shaft the largest, so a GO plug must enter and a GO ring must pass over.
  • Taylor's principle: the GO gauge checks all dimensions simultaneously over the full length; the NO-GO gauge checks one dimension at a time at individual sections.
  • The reason is functional — MMC protects assembly, which is a whole-feature property, and LMC protects local material.
  • A bowed or lobed bore accepts only \( d_{\max}=D-2\delta \); a 0.008 mm bow in a 25.010 mm bore leaves 24.994 mm, which a full-form GO plug at 25.000 correctly refuses and a two-point micrometer passes at every section.
  • Gauge-maker's tolerance \( t_g=0.10\,T \) and wear allowance \( w=0.05\,T \) are disposed inside the work zone, the wear allowance on the GO gauge only.
  • For the 21 µm hole that gives GO 25.00105 / 25.00315 and NO-GO 25.0189 / 25.0210, and a guaranteed accept band of 15.75 µm — exactly \( 0.75\,T \), the same fraction in inches.
  • The lost quarter is transferred from the manufacturer to the customer; straddling the limit instead hands it back and admits parts up to \( t_g/2 \) outside.
  • Attribute gauging is fast, unambiguous, and makes 100% inspection affordable, and a GO gauge is a more faithful test of a fit than any set of numbers.
  • It is blind between the limits: a \( 2\sigma \) drift takes rejects from 0.27% to 15.87% with no warning, where ten measured parts locate the mean to 1.11 µm. Gauge for the verdict, measure for the process.

MET 204 · Lesson 06 · Surface texture measurement

Lesson 06Surface texture measurement

Key points

  • A stylus trace contains form, waviness and roughness together; roughness is defined by the filter that extracts it, so it does not exist independently of \( \lambda_c \).
  • One sampling length is \( \lambda_c \) and the standard evaluation length is \( 5\lambda_c \).
  • The stylus filters mechanically before anything electronic does: it follows only wavelengths longer than \( \lambda_{\min}=2\pi\sqrt{ar} \), which is 6.3 µm for a 2 µm tip on a 0.5 µm amplitude.
  • \( R_a \) is the mean magnitude, \( R_q \) the root mean square, \( R_z \) and \( R_t \) the peak-to-valley measures.
  • \( R_a \) is stable and blind to shape: a sinusoid and a plateau-honed profile both at 0.80 µm stand 2.51 and 4.44 µm tall, a factor of 1.77.
  • \( R_q/R_a \) is the cheapest discriminator — 1.111 for a sinusoid, 1.253 for a random normal surface, 1.667 for the plateau profile.
  • Changing the cutoff changes the answer on an unchanged surface: 0.5 µm at 0.1 mm plus 2.0 µm at 1.6 mm reads \( R_a\ 0.318 \) at \( \lambda_c = 0.8 \) mm and 1.294 at 2.5 mm, a factor of 4.06.
  • A roughness value quoted without its cutoff is not a specification, and two parties can disagree by a factor with neither at fault.
  • Finish is a functional requirement: texture for a soft gasket, lapped for a rigid counterface, tooth for paint or adhesive, plateau with valleys for a lubricated slide.
  • \( \Lambda = h_{\min}/\sqrt{R_{q,1}^2+R_{q,2}^2} \) — a journal refinished from \( R_q\ 0.25 \) to 0.80 µm takes \( \Lambda \) from 3.82 to 2.01, out of full film and into mixed lubrication, with nothing else on the drawing changed.

MET 204 · Lesson 07 · Statistical process control I

Lesson 07Statistical process control I

Key points

  • Common-cause variation belongs to the process; special-cause variation is an assignable event. The two demand opposite responses, and telling them apart is the chart's only job.
  • Tampering — adjusting by the full deviation after every part — makes \( x_{n+1}=T-e_n+e_{n+1} \), doubling the variance and raising the standard deviation 41%.
  • A rational subgroup is chosen so that only common causes act within it, leaving special causes to show between subgroups.
  • \( \hat\sigma=\bar R/d_2 \) and the \( \bar X \) limits are \( \bar{\bar X}\pm A_2\bar R \) with \( A_2=3/(d_2\sqrt n) \) — three standard errors, packaged.
  • At \( n=5 \): \( d_2=2.326 \), \( A_2=0.577 \), \( D_4=2.114 \), \( D_3=0 \).
  • The worked chart gives limits 7.75 and 12.65 µm from \( \bar{\bar X}=10.2 \) and \( \bar R=4.25 \), with \( \hat\sigma=1.83\ \mu\mathrm{m} \) and a range-chart limit of 8.98 µm.
  • Read the range chart first: the \( \bar X \) limits are computed from \( \bar R \). A mean-only signal means the level moved and the spread did not.
  • Control limits come from the process and apply to a subgroup statistic; specification limits come from the drawing and apply to a part. At \( n=5 \) they differ by a factor of \( \sqrt5 \) in scale, so mixing them makes a chart that cannot signal.
  • In control means predictable, not good: a stable process centred outside the tolerance produces scrap stably.
  • Three-sigma limits give a false alarm every 370 points, 6.5% over 25 points; a \( 1\sigma \) shift is caught in 4.5 subgroups at \( n=5 \) and a \( 2\sigma \) shift on the next one, with sensitivity scaling as \( \sqrt n \).

MET 204 · Lesson 08 · Statistical process control II and capability

Lesson 08Statistical process control II and capability

Key points

  • Attribute charts apply the three-sigma convention to the counting distribution: \( \bar p\pm3\sqrt{\bar p(1-\bar p)/n} \) for fraction nonconforming, \( \bar c\pm3\sqrt{\bar c} \) for defects per unit.
  • At \( \bar p=0.03 \) and \( n=200 \) the lower limit truncates to zero, so the chart cannot detect an improvement; a positive limit needs \( n>9(1-\bar p)/\bar p=292 \).
  • \( C_p=(\mathrm{USL}-\mathrm{LSL})/6\hat\sigma \) asks whether the spread could fit and contains no information about centring.
  • \( C_{pk} \) takes the worse side, so \( C_{pk}\le C_p \) always, and \( k=1-C_{pk}/C_p \) is the fraction of the half-tolerance lost to aim.
  • \( C_p=1.80 \) with \( C_{pk}=0.70 \) means \( k=0.611 \), a mean \( 3.3\hat\sigma \) off centre, and 17,900 ppm outside — all of it recoverable by moving the mean.
  • \( C_{pk}=1.33 \) promises 33 ppm on the near side; the familiar 63 ppm is the two-sided total and holds only if the process is centred.
  • The 3.4 ppm figure is \( C_p=2 \) with an assumed \( 1.5\hat\sigma \) drift, so \( C_{pk}=1.5 \) and \( z=4.5 \) — a convention, not a law.
  • Stability first, capability second: \( \hat\sigma \) estimates a distribution, which an out-of-control process does not have.
  • The gauge is inside the index: \( \sigma^2_{\rm obs}=\sigma^2_{\rm proc}+\sigma^2_{\rm meas} \), and a 1.0 µm gauge on a 1.5 µm process costs 17% of \( C_p \).
  • \( \hat C_{pk}=1.33 \) from 30 parts has a 95% interval of 0.97 to 1.69, so a threshold belongs on the lower confidence limit rather than the point estimate.

MET 204 · Lesson 09 · Acceptance sampling

Lesson 09Acceptance sampling

Key points

  • A single sampling plan is two integers, \( (n,c) \), and its OC curve \( P_a(p)=\sum_{d\le c}\binom{n}{d}p^d(1-p)^{n-d} \) is the complete description of it.
  • The curve is never a step, so both parties always carry a risk: \( \alpha \) that a good lot is rejected (type I), \( \beta \) that a bad lot is accepted (type II).
  • For \( n=125,c=3 \): \( P_a \) is 0.963 at 1%, 0.481 at 3%, 0.124 at 5%; AQL 1.10%, LTPD 5.27%, discrimination ratio 4.79.
  • Discrimination is bought with sample size alone: the ratio runs 10.56, 4.79, 3.49, 2.29 for \( n= \) 50, 125, 200, 500.
  • Lot size barely matters — 125 parts from a 3% lot of 1,000 accepts at 0.4713, from 100,000 at 0.4813 — so "10% of the lot" is wrong in both directions.
  • An AQL is a property of the plan, not a target: a supplier sitting on it has a 64% chance of at least one rejection in twenty lots.
  • An accepted lot is not a good lot; sampling removes nothing from it.
  • \( \mathrm{AOQ}=pP_a(p) \) peaks at \( \mathrm{AOQL}=1.55\% \) when \( p=2.34\% \), and that ceiling holds only if rejected lots are genuinely screened and rectified.
  • For a stable process at known \( p \), the cheapest policy is one of the extremes: \( p^{*}=k_1/k_2 \), which is 0.73% at 0.40 to inspect and 55 to escape. Sampling's real home is incoming lots whose process state is unknown.
  • Destructive tests force sampling; safety consequences override the economics; and "100% inspection" at 90% efficiency still passes 0.2% of a 2% incoming rate.

MET 204 · Lesson 10 · Gauge R&R and measurement systems

Lesson 10Gauge R&R and measurement systems

Key points

  • Every reading is part plus measurement, and the variances add: \( \sigma^2_{\rm obs}=\sigma^2_{\rm part}+\sigma^2_{\rm gauge} \), with \( \sigma^2_{\rm gauge}=\sigma^2_{\rm EV}+\sigma^2_{\rm AV} \).
  • Repeatability is one operator, one part, one gauge, repeated; reproducibility is between operators — usually the work instruction rather than the people.
  • The crossed study (10 parts × 3 operators × 3 trials) uses Lesson 7's estimator: EV \( =\bar R/d_2 \), AV \( =\sqrt{(R_o/d_2)^2-\sigma^2_{\rm EV}/nr} \), PV \( =R_p/d_2 \).
  • The worked study gives EV 3.19, AV 2.59, GRR 4.11, PV 11.05 and TV 11.79 µm.
  • Two verdicts, two questions: \( \%\mathrm{GRR}=34.9\% \) against the process (unacceptable for charting) and 24.7% against a 100 µm tolerance (marginal for accept/reject).
  • Quadrature is forgiving: 34.9% of \( \sigma \) is only 12.2% of the variance, inflates \( \sigma \) by 6.7% and costs 6.3% of \( C_p \).
  • Resolution is what it destroys: \( \mathrm{ndc}=3 \) against a usual minimum of 5, and an \( \bar X \) chart 22% slower to catch a \( 1\sigma \) shift (ARL 5.47 against 4.50).
  • Read which component is large: 60% of this gauge's variance is repeatability, so fixture and method, not training. Averaging three readings gives \( \%\mathrm{GRR}=27.7\% \) and ndc 4.
  • Resolution contributes \( q^2/12 \): a 5 µm display adds 1.44 µm, a 1 µm display 0.29.
  • Guard band \( z_\gamma\sigma_{\rm GRR} \) each side — 13.5% of the tolerance here, 3.3% with a 1.0 µm gauge. Run the study before the charts, and use parts that span the real process range.

MET 204 · Lesson 11 · Quality systems and audits

Lesson 11Quality systems and audits

Key points

  • A quality system reduces to three obligations — say it, do it, prove it — and every clause of a standard serves one of them.
  • Mapped onto calibration: the written procedure and its out-of-tolerance clause; identified instruments calibrated against traceable standards; and retained certificates, budgets, investigations and recall decisions.
  • Say without do is paperwork, do without say is craft, and do without prove is indistinguishable from undone in an audit.
  • Correction fixes the product, corrective action removes the cause, preventive action acts on a cause not yet realised. A system that only corrects meets the same nonconformance forever.
  • Containment and the effectiveness check are what make a corrective action real; without the second it is a promise.
  • An out-of-tolerance instrument is a nonconformance whose containment scope was fixed by the calibration interval: \( N\approx rT/2 \), so 50,000 parts at 100,000/year on a twelve-month interval and 12,500 on a three-month one.
  • \( K(T)=C_c/T+r\lambda TC_p/2 \) minimises at \( T^{*}=\sqrt{2C_c/(r\lambda C_p)}=6.8 \) months here, costing 424 a year.
  • At the optimum the two cost terms are equal (212 each) — the fastest test of an existing interval. Twelve months costs 17% more, three months 35% more.
  • Maintenance records enter the audit trail three ways: equipment control, agreement with the calibration history, and root-cause confirmation — and a capability study never repeated after an overhaul describes a machine that no longer exists.
  • An audit is a sample: 59 records for 95% confidence of catching a 5% nonconformity, and finding nothing in 30 bounds the rate at 9.5%, close to the rule of three's \( 3/n \). "No findings" is a bound, never a zero.
