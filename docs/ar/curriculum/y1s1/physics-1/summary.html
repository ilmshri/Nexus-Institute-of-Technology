PHY 107 — Engineering Physics I — Mechanics

PHY 107 · Lesson 01 · Measurement, units, and vectors

Lesson 01Measurement, units, and vectors

Key points

  • A measurement is a contract: a value, a unit, and an honest statement of precision. Significant figures are that statement, not decoration.
  • Vectors carry direction; scalars do not. Which one a quantity is decides how it may be added.
  • Two products, two meanings. The dot product returns a scalar and answers "how much of this lies along that" — projection, work. The cross product returns a vector and answers "what axis does this turn about" — torque, area.
  • Working method: resolve into components, add componentwise, recombine at the end. Never add magnitudes of vectors that point differently.

PHY 107 · Lesson 01 · Measurement, units, and vectors · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Trigonometry — sine, cosine, tangent on right triangles, fluently.
  • Calculus I (concurrent) — derivatives arrive in Lesson 2; only algebra is needed here.
  • Scientific notation — powers of ten as the language of scale.

PHY 107 · Lesson 02 · Kinematics in one dimension

Lesson 02Kinematics in one dimension

Key points

  • Three siblings chained by calculus: position \( x(t) \) says where, velocity \( v = dx/dt \) says how fast, acceleration \( a = dv/dt \) says how the velocity itself is changing.
  • The constant-acceleration toolkit — \( v = v_0 + at \), \( x = x_0 + v_0 t + \tfrac12 at^2 \), \( v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a\Delta x \) — and the last one is the tool when time is not asked for and not known.
  • Signs carry the physics. Choose a positive direction once and keep it; deceleration is not a separate concept, only acceleration pointing against the velocity.

PHY 107 · Lesson 02 · Kinematics in one dimension · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Vectors and axes (Lesson 1) — signs come from the declared axis.
  • Derivatives and areas (Math I, concurrent) — slope and area read off graphs.
  • Unit discipline (Lesson 1) — m, m/s, m/s² kept straight throughout.

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use
Velocity\( v = dx/dt \)The slope of position–time; instantaneous, signed.
Acceleration\( a = dv/dt \)The slope of velocity–time; braking is a opposite v, not 'negative'.
Speed at a time\( v = v_0 + at \)Constant a; knowns include time.
Distance in a time\( x = v_0t + \tfrac12 at^2 \)Constant a; displacement from a clock reading.
Speed after a distance\( v^2 = v_0^2 + 2ax \)Constant a with no time given — the time-free workhorse.
Free fall\( a = g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2} \) downDropped and thrown objects, air resistance ignored.
Graph readingslope of v–t = a; area = ΔxTrend literacy — half of kinematics without an equation.

PHY 107 · Lesson 03 · Kinematics in two dimensions

Lesson 03Kinematics in two dimensions

Key points

  • Two-dimensional motion is two independent motions sharing one clock. Horizontal and vertical are separate accounts, and time is the only entry they share.
  • Projectiles: constant velocity horizontally, constant acceleration \( g \) vertically. Solve whichever axis has enough information for time, then carry that time across.
  • Relative motion is vector addition of velocities — the reason a crosswind changes a heading but not an airspeed.
  • Working method: two columns, one per axis, filled in independently.

PHY 107 · Lesson 03 · Kinematics in two dimensions · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • 1-D kinematics (Lesson 2) — the constant-acceleration kit, fluently.
  • Vector components (Lesson 1) — splitting a launch velocity by angle.
  • The shared-clock idea — time is the only variable both axes see.

Glossary

Velocity split\( v_x = v_0\cos\theta,\ v_y = v_0\sin\theta \)Angled launches; the components live separate lives after this line.

PHY 107 · Lesson 04 · Newton's laws of motion

Lesson 04Newton's laws of motion

Key points

  • First law: velocity is constant unless a net force acts. So anything at rest or cruising steadily has balanced forces, and the engineering question is always "balanced by what?"
  • Second law: \( \mathbf{F}_{net} = m\mathbf{a} \) — the vector sum of everything acting produces acceleration in its own direction, scaled inversely by the mass.
  • Third law: forces come in equal and opposite pairs acting on different bodies. Pairs acting on the same body never cancel, because they do not exist.
  • Mass versus weight, permanently: mass is in kilograms and is a property of the object; weight is the gravitational force \( W = mg \) in newtons and is local to the gravity producing it.

PHY 107 · Lesson 04 · Newton's laws of motion · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Free-body diagrams (Statics, Lesson 3 there) — the isolation discipline, unchanged.
  • Vector components (Lesson 1) — the second law is applied by axis.
  • Kinematics (Lessons 2–3) — a found here feeds the motion equations there.

PHY 107 · Lesson 05 · Applying Newton's laws

Lesson 05Applying Newton's laws

Key points

  • Friction joins the free-body diagram: static friction up to its threshold, kinetic friction once sliding starts.
  • Ropes and pulleys give shared fates: bodies connected by an inextensible rope share the magnitude of their acceleration, which is what makes the simultaneous equations solvable.
  • Friction is not only a thief. It is the transmitter of motion — the reason a driven wheel moves a vehicle and a belt turns a pulley at all.
  • Working method: one free-body diagram per body, axes aligned with the motion, then solve the set together.

PHY 107 · Lesson 05 · Applying Newton's laws · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • The second law by axis (Lesson 4) — the engine this lesson feeds.
  • Contact friction as an idea — that a surface resists sliding with a force proportional to the normal load. The model itself is supplied here: an adaptive static ceiling and a fixed kinetic value. Engineering Statics later builds equilibrium problems on it.
  • Incline components — \( mg\sin\theta \) along, \( mg\cos\theta \) into the slope.

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use
Static friction\( F_s \le \mu_s N \)Adaptive grip before sliding; equality only at the slip threshold.
Kinetic friction\( F_k = \mu_k N \)Fixed drag once sliding; always below the static ceiling.
Normal on an incline\( N = mg\cos\theta \)Never assume N = W off the horizontal.
Slide condition\( \tan\theta > \mu_s \)The incline holds or releases on this comparison alone.
Rope constraintone tension, one acceleration magnitudeIdeal cords make multi-body systems solvable.

Limit audits\( \mu \to 0 \), \( a \to 0 \)Answers must collapse to frictionless and static cases.

PHY 107 · Lesson 06 · Work and energy

Lesson 06Work and energy

Key points

  • Work is energy in transit by force: \( W = Fd\cos\theta \), where only the component of force along the motion counts. Force perpendicular to motion does no work.
  • The work–energy theorem — net work equals the change in kinetic energy — shortcuts dynamics whenever the question does not mention time.
  • \( KE = \tfrac12 mv^2 \). That squared velocity matters more than any other exponent in safety: doubling speed quadruples the energy to be absorbed.
  • Power \( P = W/t \), in watts, is what actually sizes machines — a motor is bought for its rate of doing work, not its total work.

PHY 107 · Lesson 06 · Work and energy · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Dot product (Lesson 1) — work is its flagship application.
  • Second law (Lesson 4) — the theorem is its integral along the path.
  • Kinematics (Lesson 2) — states and speeds, before and after.

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use
Work\( W = Fd\cos\theta \)Force through distance, along-motion component only; signed.
Kinetic energy\( KE = \tfrac12 mv^2 \)The energy of motion; the \( v^2 \) drives all impact scaling.
Work–energy theorem\( W_{net} = \Delta KE \)State-to-state shortcut past acceleration bookkeeping.
Zero-work forces\( \perp \) to motion, or no motionNormal forces, centripetal pulls, static holds — effort without joules.
Power\( P = W/t = Fv \)The rate that sizes motors, hoists, and drives.
Hoist power\( P = mgv \)Steady lifting; the crane-sizing one-liner.
Kilowatt-hour\( 1\ \mathrm{kWh} = 3.6\ \mathrm{MJ} \)Energy on the invoice; power is the rate, energy is the bill.

PHY 107 · Lesson 07 · Potential energy and conservation

Lesson 07Potential energy and conservation

Key points

  • Potential energy is stored work: gravitational \( mgh \), elastic \( \tfrac12 kx^2 \).
  • Conservation: with no friction, kinetic plus potential energy is constant. The bookkeeping is between states — before and after — and never needs the path.
  • \( v = \sqrt{2gh} \) answers speed-from-height in a single line, with mass cancelling entirely.
  • With friction the ledger still balances. Nothing is lost, only transferred: the loss term is the friction force times the distance over which it acted.

PHY 107 · Lesson 07 · Potential energy and conservation · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Work and the theorem (Lesson 6) — potential energy is work pre-paid.
  • Free fall (Lesson 2) — \( v = \sqrt{2gh} \) should already feel familiar.
  • Sign discipline — energy gained somewhere is energy paid from somewhere.

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use
Gravitational PE\( PE = mgh \)Stored lift; only differences matter — declare the reference once.
Spring PE\( PE = \tfrac12 kx^2 \)Banked compression or stretch; x from the free length.
Conservative forcepath-independent workGravity and springs repay in full; friction does not qualify.
Conservation\( KE_1 + PE_1 = KE_2 + PE_2 \)No non-conservative work between states.
Speed from height\( v = \sqrt{2gh} \)Drop from rest; mass cancels.
With losses\( E_1 = E_2 + F_f d \)Friction's collection routed to heat, explicitly.

PHY 107 · Lesson 08 · Momentum, impulse, and collisions

Lesson 08Momentum, impulse, and collisions

Key points

  • Momentum \( p = mv \) is motion's currency, and the second law's deeper statement is that force is the rate of change of momentum.
  • Impulse is force integrated over contact time, and equals the change in momentum. This is why crumple zones and longer stopping times reduce force for the same speed change.
  • Momentum is conserved for an isolated system whether or not the collision is gentle.
  • Collisions: momentum is always kept; kinetic energy may not be. Elastic collisions keep both; perfectly inelastic ones stick together and lose the most energy available.

PHY 107 · Lesson 08 · Momentum, impulse, and collisions · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Second and third laws (Lesson 4) — conservation is their joint consequence.
  • Vectors and signs (Lesson 1) — momentum is direction-carrying.
  • Kinetic energy (Lesson 6) — the second, separate audit in collisions.

Glossary

Impulse–momentum theorem\( F\,\Delta t = \Delta p \)Trading force against time at fixed momentum change.
Conservation\( \Sigma p \) constant, no net external forceCollisions, couplings, separations — the first equation written.
Perfectly inelasticbodies couple; max KE lossCoupling wagons, catching parts, sticking impacts.

PHY 107 · Lesson 09 · Rotation I: kinematics and torque

Lesson 09Rotation I: kinematics and torque

Key points

  • The angular vocabulary mirrors the linear one: angle \( \theta \), angular velocity \( \omega = d\theta/dt \), angular acceleration \( \alpha \). Radians are the natural unit because they make arc length \( s = \theta r \) work with no conversion factor.
  • The rolling connection \( v = \omega r \) and \( a_t = \alpha r \) runs every belt, chain and gear train you will meet.
  • Torque is force's rotational agent — force times perpendicular distance from the axis. On a belt drive the torque transmitted is the tension difference times radius, because both runs pull in opposition.
  • The constant-\( \alpha \) toolkit is the Lesson 2 toolkit with letters swapped.

PHY 107 · Lesson 09 · Rotation I: kinematics and torque · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Linear kinematics (Lesson 2) — the toolkit transfers letter-for-letter.
  • Vectors and the perpendicular component (Lesson 1) — all that torque needs. The moment of a force is defined in this lesson as force times perpendicular arm; Engineering Statics takes it up afterwards for bodies in equilibrium.
  • Radian measure (Math I) — arc = radius × angle, the native unit.

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use
Angular kinematics\( \omega = \omega_0 + \alpha t \), etc.Lesson 2's kit with \( \theta, \omega, \alpha \) — same selection rules.
rpm ↔ rad/s\( \omega = 2\pi N/60 \)The border crossing every machine problem makes; 1450 rpm ≈ 152 rad/s.
Rim speed\( v = \omega r \)Belts, wheels, and cutting speeds; radians only.
Tangential acceleration\( a_t = \alpha r \)How rim speed changes during spin-up and coast-down.
Torque\( \tau = Fr\sin\theta \)Force's turning agent; N·m.
Belt-drive torque\( \tau = (T_1 - T_2)\,r \)The tension DIFFERENCE does the work — both runs pull.
Speed ratio\( \omega_1 r_1 = \omega_2 r_2 \)Shared rim speed across pulleys and meshing gears.

PHY 107 · Lesson 10 · Rotation II: dynamics and angular momentum

Lesson 10Rotation II: dynamics and angular momentum

Key points

  • Moment of inertia is rotation's mass, but unlike mass it depends on where the material sits, not merely how much there is.
  • A spinning body banks kinetic energy \( KE = \tfrac12 I\omega^2 \), and the squared \( \omega \) makes speed the cheapest way to store it — the flywheel's whole principle.
  • Angular momentum \( L = I\omega \) is momentum's rotational sibling, conserved whenever no external torque acts. Pulling mass inward lowers \( I \) and must raise \( \omega \).
  • \( \Sigma \tau = I\alpha \) is the rotational second law, and the working method is identical to the linear one.

PHY 107 · Lesson 10 · Rotation II: dynamics and angular momentum · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Rotational kinematics (Lesson 9) — \( \omega, \alpha \), and the rpm border.
  • Second law and energy (Lessons 4, 6) — every law here is a translation.
  • The idea of a distance-squared weighting — the mass moment of inertia \( I=\sum m_ir_i^2 \) is derived in this lesson from the kinetic energy of a rotating body. The area moment of inertia met later in Engineering Statics shares the square and differs in the integrand.

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use
Rotational second law\( \tau = I\alpha \)Net torque about the axis produces angular acceleration.
Moment of inertia\( I = \int r^2\,dm \)Mass times distance SQUARED; distribution decides.
Standard shapesdisc \( \tfrac12 mr^2 \); hoop \( mr^2 \)The two benchmarks bracketing most machine rotors.
Rotational KE\( \tfrac12 I\omega^2 \)Flywheel storage; \( \omega^2 \) makes speed the cheap lever.
Rolling energy\( \tfrac12 mv^2 + \tfrac12 I\omega^2 \)Rolling bodies pay both accounts from one height budget.
Angular momentum\( L = I\omega \); \( \tau = dL/dt \)Conserved without external torque; the I–ω trade.
Coast-down reading\( \alpha = \tau_{friction}/I \)Spin-down time measures drag torque — a field diagnostic.

PHY 107 · Lesson 11 · Oscillations first contact

Lesson 11Oscillations first contact

Key points

  • The restoring recipe: a force proportional to displacement and directed against it produces simple harmonic motion. The spring–mass system \( F = -kx \) is the archetype.
  • \( \omega_n = \sqrt{k/m} \): stiffness accelerates the cycle, mass slows it. Crucially the natural frequency is a property of the machine, not of whatever disturbs it.
  • The pendulum repeats the recipe with gravity as the spring: \( T = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g} \), with mass absent entirely.
  • Signature quantities: period \( T = 2\pi/\omega_n \), frequency \( f = 1/T \) in hertz, and energy sloshing between kinetic and potential twice per cycle.
  • Resonance, previewed: driving a machine near its own natural frequency is how machines shake themselves apart, and is why this lesson exists in a mechanical curriculum.

PHY 107 · Lesson 11 · Oscillations first contact · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Newton's second law (Lesson 4) — SHM is F = ma with a restoring F.
  • Energy conservation (Lesson 7) — the KE–PE slosh at constant total.
  • Radian fluency (Lesson 9) — \( \omega \), f, and T, converted consciously.

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use
Restoring force\( F = -kx \)Proportional pull toward equilibrium — the oscillation ingredient.
Natural frequency\( \omega_n = \sqrt{k/m} \)The machine's own beat; stiffness up, mass down.
Period and frequency\( T = 2\pi/\omega_n,\ f = 1/T \)The 2π border between rad/s and Hz.
Pendulum\( T = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g} \)Gravity as the spring; mass cancels, small swings only.
SHM motion\( x = A\sin(\omega_n t) \)Amplitude from the start; frequency from the system.
