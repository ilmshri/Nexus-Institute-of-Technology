FLD 203 — Fluid Mechanics

FLD 203 · Lesson 01 · Fluid properties

Lesson 01Fluid properties

Key points

  • A fluid is defined by behaviour, not phase: it cannot resist shear at rest, so any shear stress at all makes it deform continuously.
  • Viscosity is the constant of proportionality in Newton's law of viscosity, \( \tau=\mu\,du/dy \) — resistance to shear rate, not to shear.
  • Kinematic viscosity \( \nu=\mu/\rho \) is what appears in the Reynolds number, so it is the one that governs flow regime.
  • Temperature moves the two phases opposite ways: liquid viscosity falls sharply as it warms, gas viscosity rises.
  • That opposite response is a molecular-mechanism difference — cohesion in liquids, momentum exchange in gases.
  • Establish the properties at the actual operating temperature before any calculation; a room-temperature viscosity can be wrong by an order of magnitude.

Assumed knowledge

  • Stress — force per area, from Statics/Strength.
  • Derivatives — the velocity gradient \(du/dy\).

FLD 203 · Lesson 02 · Pressure and fluid statics

Lesson 02Pressure and fluid statics

Key points

  • Pressure at a point acts equally in all directions, and in a static fluid it varies only with depth: \( p=\rho gh \).
  • Horizontal position and container shape are irrelevant — the hydrostatic paradox follows directly.
  • Gauge pressure is measured relative to atmosphere, absolute relative to vacuum, and mixing them is the most common error in the subject.
  • Any equation containing a gas law needs absolute pressure; most instruments read gauge.
  • Manometers apply the hydrostatic law directly: work through the fluid column by column, adding going down and subtracting going up.
  • A manometer's sensitivity is set by the density difference, which is why inclined and differential designs exist.

FLD 203 · Lesson 02 · Pressure and fluid statics · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Fluid properties (Lesson 1) — density is the key input.
  • Force and pressure — force per area.
  • Integration of a gradient — \(dp/dz=-\rho g\).

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use

Gauge pressure\( p_{gauge}=p_{abs}-p_{atm} \)Structural load across a wall.

FLD 203 · Lesson 03 · Flow description, Reynolds number, and continuity

Lesson 03Flow description, Reynolds number, and continuity

Key points

  • Flow is described by streamlines, pathlines and streaklines — identical in steady flow, different otherwise.
  • The Reynolds number \( Re=\rho VD/\mu \) is the master number: the ratio of inertial to viscous forces.
  • It decides regime, and in pipe flow the transition sits near \( Re=2300 \).
  • Laminar flow is orderly and predictable analytically; turbulent flow mixes aggressively and needs correlations.
  • Continuity is mass conservation: \( \rho_1A_1V_1=\rho_2A_2V_2 \), reducing to \( A_1V_1=A_2V_2 \) when density is constant.
  • Continuity is what makes a nozzle accelerate flow, and it is the first equation to write in almost every problem.

FLD 203 · Lesson 03 · Flow description, Reynolds number, and continuity · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Kinematic viscosity (Lesson 1) — the \( \nu \) in \(Re\).
  • Circle area — \(A=\pi D^2/4\).
  • Mass conservation — nothing accumulates in steady flow.

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use

Mass flow rate\( \dot m=\rho AV \)Balances, compressible flow.
Reynolds number\( Re=VD/\nu \)Laminar vs turbulent regime.

FLD 203 · Lesson 04 · Bernoulli, energy equation, pipe flow, and pump curves

Lesson 04Bernoulli, energy equation, pipe flow, and pump curves

Key points

  • Bernoulli's equation is energy per unit weight along a streamline: pressure head, velocity head and elevation head sum to a constant.
  • It trades the three against each other — faster flow means lower pressure, which is the whole basis of lift, venturis and carburettors.
  • Its assumptions are strict: steady, incompressible, frictionless, along a streamline, no machine in between.
  • Violate any of them and the equation is simply wrong, not merely approximate.
  • The real energy equation restores what Bernoulli discards: head loss \( h_L \) and machine head \( h_p \) or \( h_t \).
  • That extended form is what actually sizes pumps and pipework for the rest of the course.

FLD 203 · Lesson 04 · Bernoulli, energy equation, pipe flow, and pump curves · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Hydrostatic head (Lesson 2) — the pressure/elevation terms.
  • Energy conservation — the balance being written.

Glossary

FLD 203 · Lesson 05 · Momentum analysis

Lesson 05Momentum analysis

Key points

  • Energy methods give speeds and pressures but never forces — a bend can be analysed by Bernoulli and still tell you nothing about its anchor loads.
  • The momentum equation supplies them: \( \sum F=\dot m(V_{out}-V_{in}) \), applied to a chosen control volume.
  • Momentum is a vector equation, so it is written per axis and the signs are the analysis.
  • Drawing the control volume and marking every force crossing it is the step that decides whether the answer is right.
  • A 180° reversal produces roughly twice the force of a 90° bend, because the momentum change is twice as large.
  • These are the loads that size thrust blocks, pipe anchors and nozzle reaction supports.

FLD 203 · Lesson 05 · Momentum analysis · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Newton's second law — momentum rate equals net force.
  • Vector components — the equation is directional.

FLD 203 · Lesson 06 · Dimensional analysis and similarity

Lesson 06Dimensional analysis and similarity

Key points

  • Making variables dimensionless collapses a many-variable problem into a few groups, cutting the experiments needed enormously.
  • Buckingham Pi says a relation among \( n \) variables with \( k \) dimensions reduces to \( n-k \) dimensionless groups.
  • The named groups are not arbitrary: Reynolds, Froude, Mach, Euler and Weber each compare a specific pair of forces.
  • Similarity requires geometric, kinematic and dynamic likeness — matching shape alone is not a model test.
  • Dynamic similarity means matching the governing dimensionless group, which is why a model is run at matched \( Re \), not matched speed.
  • Matching every group at once is usually impossible, so the engineering judgement is deciding which one governs.

Assumed knowledge

  • Reynolds number (Lesson 3) — the archetypal group.

FLD 203 · Lesson 07 · Laminar and turbulent pipe flow in depth

Lesson 07Laminar and turbulent pipe flow in depth

Key points

  • Real pipe flow loses head to friction, given by Darcy–Weisbach: \( h_f=f\frac{L}{D}\frac{V^2}{2g} \).
  • The velocity squared is why oversizing a line pays back so quickly — halving velocity quarters the friction loss.
  • In laminar flow \( f=64/Re \) exactly, independent of roughness, because the wall is buried under the viscous layer.
  • In turbulent flow \( f \) depends on both \( Re \) and relative roughness, read from the Moody chart or Colebrook equation.
  • At high \( Re \) the flow becomes fully rough and \( f \) stops depending on \( Re \) altogether.
  • Roughness only matters once turbulence has thinned the viscous sublayer enough to expose it.

  • Relative roughness — \( \varepsilon/D \) for turbulent pipes.

FLD 203 · Lesson 08 · Piping networks and valves

Lesson 08Piping networks and valves

Key points

  • Fittings, bends, valves and entries lose head through \( h_L=K\frac{V^2}{2g} \).
  • They are called minor losses, and in a short, fitting-dense run they routinely dominate the straight-pipe friction.
  • Equivalent length restates a \( K \) value as a length of pipe, which makes hand estimation quick.
  • In series, flow is common and losses add; in parallel, head loss is common and flows divide.
  • A partly closed valve has an enormous \( K \) — which is exactly how throttling control works, and why it is thermodynamically wasteful.
  • Network solving is bookkeeping on those two rules plus continuity at every junction.

FLD 203 · Lesson 09 · Flow measurement

Lesson 09Flow measurement

Key points

  • Most flow measurement is really pressure measurement, converted through Bernoulli.
  • A Pitot tube reads stagnation minus static pressure, giving velocity as \( V=\sqrt{2\Delta p/\rho} \) — a point velocity, not a flow rate.
  • Venturi and orifice meters create a deliberate contraction and read the pressure drop across it.
  • A discharge coefficient corrects the ideal result for real effects; it is not optional.
  • The trade-off is direct: a venturi recovers most of its pressure drop and costs more, an orifice plate is cheap and permanently wasteful.
  • Because \( \Delta p\propto Q^2 \), these meters have poor turndown — accuracy collapses at low flow.

FLD 203 · Lesson 10 · Turbomachinery fundamentals

Lesson 10Turbomachinery fundamentals

Key points

  • A pump adds head, and head is the honest unit because it is independent of fluid density.
  • The operating point is where the pump curve crosses the system curve — neither alone determines it.
  • Throttling moves the system curve, not the pump curve, which is why throttling control burns energy as loss.
  • The affinity laws scale performance with speed: \( Q\propto N \), \( H\propto N^2 \), \( P\propto N^3 \).
  • That cubic power law is the entire economic case for variable-speed drives on pumps and fans.
  • Cavitation occurs when local pressure falls to vapour pressure; NPSH available must exceed NPSH required, and it is a suction-side problem that no discharge change will fix.

FLD 203 · Lesson 11 · Compressible flow first contact

Lesson 11Compressible flow first contact

Key points

  • Density stops being constant once Mach number rises above roughly 0.3 — below that, incompressible treatment is honest.
  • The speed of sound \( c=\sqrt{kRT} \) depends on temperature alone for an ideal gas, not on pressure.
  • Mach number \( M=V/c \) sorts the regimes: subsonic, sonic, supersonic.
  • Sound is how a flow is warned of what lies downstream, so supersonic flow cannot be warned at all — which is why shocks exist.
  • A normal shock is an abrupt, irreversible jump: pressure and temperature rise, velocity drops to subsonic, and entropy increases.
  • A converging-diverging nozzle chokes at the throat and needs the diverging section to go supersonic — the opposite of subsonic intuition.

FLD 203 · Lesson 11 · Compressible flow first contact · fundamentals

Assumed knowledge

  • Bernoulli's limits (Lesson 4) — where incompressibility fails.
  • Absolute temperature — the speed of sound needs kelvin.
