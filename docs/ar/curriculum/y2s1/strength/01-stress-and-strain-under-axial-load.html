SOM 201 · Lesson 01 of 11 · Year 2 · Semester 1

Stress and strain under axial load

Normal stress and strain defined from first principles; the tensile curve reread as an engineer's contract — modulus, yield, ultimate, ductility. Allowable stress and factor of safety as institutional honesty. Saint-Venant's caution about where formulas apply.

المحاضرة

After this lesson you can answer:
  • A 16 mm bar carries 45 kN: compute the stress and the safety factor against 355 MPa yield.
  • Engineering versus true stress: which keeps rising after necking, and why?
  • Why do aluminum alloys need the 0.2% offset construction to define yield at all?

01 · Stress: force made local

A load of a few kilonewtons means nothing to a material until it is spread over an area. Normal stress \( \sigma = P/A \) is that intensity — force per unit area, in pascals (N/m²), or in engineering practice megapascals. A 100 kN pull on a 25 mm round bar (\( A=\tfrac{\pi}{4}d^2=490.9\ \text{mm}^2 \)) is \( \sigma=100{,}000/490.9\times10^{-6}\approx204\ \text{MPa} \). The same force on a thicker bar is a gentler stress. Stress, not force, is what a material feels, and it is the quantity every allowable is written against.

02 · Strain: deformation made dimensionless

Normal strain \( \varepsilon = \delta/L \) is the stretch per unit length — a pure number. It divides out the specimen size so that a 1 mm stretch in a 1 m bar (\( \varepsilon=0.001 \)) and a 2 mm stretch in a 2 m bar describe the same material state. This normalisation is why stress and strain, not force and elongation, are the axes of the material's fingerprint: they belong to the material, not to the part.

03 · Hooke's law and the elastic contract

Over the initial straight portion of the tensile curve, stress is proportional to strain: \( \sigma = E\varepsilon \). The slope \(E\) is Young's modulus — steel's is about 200 GPa — a stiffness the material carries regardless of shape. Combining the three relations gives the workhorse deformation formula \( \delta = \dfrac{PL}{AE} \). An 80 kN load on a 2 m steel bar of 20 mm diameter stretches it \( \delta=\dfrac{80{,}000\times2}{314\times10^{-6}\times200\times10^9} \approx2.55\ \text{mm} \). Longer and softer stretches more; thicker and stiffer stretches less — all four factors sit in one fraction.

04 · Reading the tensile curve as a contract

The tensile test is the material's signed contract. The proportional limit ends Hooke's law; the yield strength marks the onset of permanent deformation — the number most designs are kept below; the ultimate strength is the peak the specimen can carry; and the strain at fracture measures ductility, the warning a part gives before it breaks. Design keeps the working stress a chosen margin below yield, expressed as a factor of safety \( \text{FoS}=\sigma_{\text{yield}}/\sigma_{\text{allow}} \): a 250 MPa yield held to a 160 MPa allowable is a FoS of about 1.56.

05 · Why this lesson is the foundation of the course

Every later topic — torsion, bending, combined loading, buckling, fatigue — is this same logic applied to a different stress: define the stress from the load and geometry, relate it to a strain through an elastic law, and compare against an allowable drawn from the tensile contract. The stress–strain diagram is therefore not one lesson among many but the template for all of them. Master reading it — where the straight part ends, how much warning the ductility gives, what margin the factor of safety buys — and the rest of Strength of Materials becomes variations on a theme you already understand. The habit of converting a raw force into a local stress, and only then judging safety, is the single most important reflex the course installs.

الأسس

What this lesson assumes

  • Statics — internal force from a free-body cut precedes any stress.
  • Areas of sections — \(A=\tfrac{\pi}{4}d^2\) for round bars.
  • Unit discipline — MPa = N/mm²; keep a consistent system.

Glossary

TermEquationWhen to use
Normal stress\( \sigma=P/A \)Axial force spread over a cross-section.
Normal strain\( \varepsilon=\delta/L \)Size-independent measure of stretch.
Hooke's law\( \sigma=E\varepsilon \)Elastic (straight-line) region only.
Axial deformation\( \delta=PL/AE \)Elongation of a uniform bar.
Factor of safety\( \sigma_y/\sigma_{allow} \)Margin held below yield.

Problem 1 of 8 · solve, then check

Axial stress

Problem. A 100 kN load acts on a round bar of 25 mm diameter. Find the normal stress.

Solution. \( A=\tfrac{\pi}{4}(25)^2=490.9\ \text{mm}^2 \). \( \sigma=P/A=100{,}000/490.9=203.7\ \text{MPa} \). Answer: \(\approx204\) MPa. Check: N/mm² = MPa, so no extra conversion.

Problem 2 of 8 · solve, then check

Axial stretch

Problem. An 80 kN load stretches a 2 m steel bar (d=20 mm, E=200 GPa). Find the elongation.

Solution. \( A=\tfrac{\pi}{4}(20)^2=314\ \text{mm}^2=314\times10^{-6}\,\text{m}^2 \). \( \delta=\dfrac{PL}{AE}=\dfrac{80{,}000\times2}{314\times10^{-6}\times200\times10^9}=2.55\times10^{-3}\,\text{m} \). Answer: \(\approx2.55\) mm.

Problem 3 of 8 · solve, then check

Factor of safety

Problem. A steel of 250 MPa yield is used at an allowable stress of 160 MPa. State the factor of safety.

Solution. \( \text{FoS}=\sigma_y/\sigma_{allow}=250/160=1.56 \). Answer: \(\approx1.56\). Check: FoS>1 means the allowable sits below yield, as required.

Problem 4 of 8 · multiple choice

Normal stress is defined as:

Stress is force per unit area, \(P/A\). \(\delta/L\) is strain; the others are dimensionally wrong.

Problem 5 of 8 · multiple choice

Young's modulus \(E\) is the slope of:

\(E=\sigma/\varepsilon\) is the slope of the straight, elastic portion only. Beyond yield the curve bends and \(E\) no longer applies.

Problem 6 of 8 · multiple choice

Doubling only the length \(L\) of an axially loaded bar changes its elongation \( \delta=PL/AE \) by:

\(\delta\propto L\): twice the length, twice the stretch under the same load. Area and modulus are unchanged.

Problem 7 of 8 · multiple choice

The yield strength is important because it marks:

Below yield, deformation is (nearly) recoverable; above it, permanent set begins. Most designs keep working stress below yield.

Problem 8 of 8 · multiple choice

Strain \( \varepsilon=\delta/L \) has units of:

Length over length cancels — strain is a pure number. Stress carries the pascals, not strain.

Unit versatility

The theory above is in SI. Engineering practice also runs in US/Imperial and CGS — convert each quantity, then check. Get one wrong and the engine hands you a fresh variant of the same conversion.
