ELX 205 · Lesson 01 · Semiconductors, diodes, and power supplies

Lesson 01Semiconductors, diodes, and power supplies

Key points

  • A diode conducts one way, which is what makes rectification possible; a bridge rectifier uses both half-cycles.
  • A capacitor then holds the peak between charging pulses, converting pulsating DC toward steady DC.
  • Ripple is the droop the capacitor allows between refills, and it grows with load current and shrinks with capacitance.
  • A regulator holds output constant against both line and load variation; its datasheet dropout voltage sets the minimum input it needs.
  • Regulator power dissipation is the voltage it drops times the current it passes — the number that sizes the heatsink.
  • 24 VDC is the industrial standard because it is high enough for noise immunity over long runs and low enough to be touch-safe.

ELX 205 · Lesson 02 · Transistors and switching

Lesson 02Transistors and switching

Key points

  • A transistor used as a switch has two intended states: fully off with no current, and fully saturated with almost no voltage across it.
  • In both states the power dissipated is small, because either current or voltage is near zero.
  • The linear region between them is where both are significant at once, so that is where the heat is made.
  • Switching fast is therefore about minimising time spent in transit, not about speed for its own sake.
  • An inductive load such as a relay coil needs a flyback diode, or turning it off generates a destructive voltage spike.
  • Solid-state relays switch silently with no contacts to wear but leak slightly and drop voltage; electromechanical relays isolate truly and switch cleanly but wear out.

ELX 205 · Lesson 03 · Op-amps and signal conditioning

Lesson 03Op-amps and signal conditioning

Key points

  • Op-amps amplify, filter and buffer, and in instrumentation the differential configuration is the one that matters.
  • An instrumentation amplifier amplifies the difference between its inputs while rejecting what is common to both.
  • Common-mode rejection ratio measures that rejection, and it is the primary defence against plant noise.
  • Noise picked up on a twisted pair appears almost equally on both conductors, so it is common-mode and gets rejected.
  • That is how a thermocouple's few tens of microvolts survive a plant full of motors and drives.
  • Signal conditioning also sets bandwidth: filtering out what is not signal is part of the measurement, not an afterthought.

ELX 205 · Lesson 04 · Digital logic and interfaces

Lesson 04Digital logic and interfaces

Key points

  • Digital logic works in defined levels, with a deliberate gap between the highest valid low and the lowest valid high — the noise margin.
  • Gates combine those levels; combinational logic is described by truth tables and Boolean algebra.
  • A PLC discrete input is a logic-level detector, so it reports presence or absence, not magnitude.
  • Sinking and sourcing describe which side of the load carries the switched connection, and mismatching them is the classic commissioning fault.
  • A sourcing output must be paired with a sinking input, and the convention differs by region and vendor.
  • Getting it right on paper before wiring is far cheaper than finding it at the terminal strip.

ELX 205 · Lesson 05 · Sensors on a production line

Lesson 05Sensors on a production line

Key points

  • The 4–20 mA current loop is an engineering decision, not a convention: current is the same everywhere in a series loop, so wire resistance does not corrupt the reading.
  • That immunity to voltage drop is why it survives long plant cable runs where a voltage signal would not.
  • The live zero is the key feature — 4 mA means "zero measurand", while 0 mA means broken wire.
  • A voltage signal cannot distinguish those two states at all, which is a safety difference, not a convenience.
  • The transfer function is linear: current maps to the calibrated range, so any value converts by simple proportion.
  • Loop-powered transmitters draw their supply from the same two wires, which is why the budget for loop resistance has to be checked.

ELX 205 · Lesson 06 · Temperature measurement in depth

Lesson 06Temperature measurement in depth

Key points

  • RTDs are resistance-versus-temperature devices, accurate, stable and nearly linear, with Pt100 the industrial standard.
  • They need excitation current, which self-heats the element — so the sensor can warm what it is measuring.
  • Three- and four-wire connections cancel lead resistance, which otherwise reads as a temperature offset.
  • Thermocouples generate a voltage from a junction of dissimilar metals; they are wide-range, rugged, cheap and less accurate.
  • They measure a difference, so a cold-junction reference is mandatory and matched extension wire must be used all the way.
  • A thermowell protects the sensor and lets it be replaced live, at the cost of a real response lag — an installation decision, not a detail.

ELX 205 · Lesson 07 · Pressure and level measurement in depth

Lesson 07Pressure and level measurement in depth

Key points

  • Pressure is measured as gauge, absolute or differential, and naming which is the first step of any specification.
  • Hydrostatic level measurement infers level from \( p=\rho gh \), so it depends on density and reads wrongly if the fluid changes.
  • A closed, pressurised tank needs a differential transmitter to subtract the vapour-space pressure.
  • The reference leg fills with condensate over time, which is exactly why the wet leg is deliberately filled and accounted for.
  • Zero suppression and elevation correct for a transmitter mounted above or below the tapping point.
  • Specify range, over-range limit, wetted materials, process connection and accuracy together — a transmitter is not specified by range alone.

ELX 205 · Lesson 08 · Vibration and speed sensors

Lesson 08Vibration and speed sensors

Key points

  • Vibration is measured because rotating machinery announces its faults through it long before failure.
  • An accelerometer's piezoelectric element outputs charge proportional to acceleration, integrated to velocity or displacement as needed.
  • Velocity is the usual overall health measure across mid frequencies; displacement suits low frequencies and acceleration high ones.
  • Mounting sets the usable bandwidth: stud mounting gives the widest, adhesive less, and a magnet or handheld probe far less.
  • A handheld reading is therefore not comparable with a stud-mounted one, which matters when trending data over time.
  • Proximity probes measure shaft displacement directly, which is what journal-bearing machines actually need; encoders and tachometers supply the speed reference every spectrum is interpreted against.

ELX 205 · Lesson 09 · Analog-to-digital and sampling

Lesson 09Analog-to-digital and sampling

Key points

  • An ADC samples in time and quantises in amplitude, and both introduce error.
  • Resolution sets the quantisation step — the smallest change the converter can represent.
  • Nyquist requires sampling at more than twice the highest frequency present in the signal.
  • Undersampling does not lose the high frequency, it disguises it: aliasing reports a false low frequency that looks entirely genuine.
  • Nothing downstream can undo it, because the data no longer contain the information needed to tell truth from alias.
  • An analogue anti-aliasing filter before the converter is the only fix — it must remove the content before sampling, not after.

ELX 205 · Lesson 10 · Signal transmission and noise

Lesson 10Signal transmission and noise

Key points

  • Ground is not one potential everywhere: separated points differ by real millivolts from currents in the structure.
  • A ground loop forms when a signal circuit is earthed at more than one point, so that difference is injected straight into the measurement.
  • The result is noise proportional to plant activity, which is why it appears and disappears with other equipment starting.
  • The fix is single-point grounding: earth the shield at one end only, normally the control-system end.
  • Shielding intercepts capacitive coupling; twisting the pair defeats magnetic coupling — the two techniques address different mechanisms.
  • Galvanic isolation breaks the loop entirely and is worth its cost on long runs, on high-noise areas and across separate earthing systems.

ELX 205 · Lesson 11 · Sensor selection and installation project

Lesson 11Sensor selection and installation project

Key points

  • Sensor selection starts from the measurement itself: quantity, range, required accuracy and how fast it must respond.
  • Then the interface: signal type, loop power, sinking or sourcing, and what the controller can actually accept.
  • Then the environment: temperature, vibration, washdown, hazardous-area classification and ingress protection.
  • Then installation: mounting location, thermowell or direct, orientation, straight-run requirements and access for calibration.
  • A sensor installed in the wrong place is wrong however good its specification — position is part of the measurement.
  • Document range, calibration, tag number, wiring and rationale so the next engineer inherits the reasoning, not just the hardware.
